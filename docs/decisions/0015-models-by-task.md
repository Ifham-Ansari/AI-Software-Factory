# 0015: Choosing models by task, from every free provider

**Status:** accepted · 2026-11-22

**Context.** The built-in engine ([0014](0014-builtin-engine.md)) used each key's single best model for every run. That spends the scarcest allowance on everything: the largest free models usually have the smallest limits (Groq's biggest model gets 1,000 requests and 200k tokens a day, its 8B model 14,400 requests), so sizing a request or a one-line change used the same model as writing the architecture. The owner wants as much free capacity as possible for larger projects, with the right model for each task and no thinking about API usage. Free tiers also changed in 2026: GitHub Models was retired (30 July 2026), Cerebras ended its ongoing free tier, Gemini Pro left the free tier, and new providers appeared.

**Decision.**

* **More providers.** Presets for Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter, NVIDIA API Catalog, Mistral, Z.ai, Cloudflare Workers AI, SambaNova, Ollama Cloud, Kilo Gateway, OVHcloud AI Endpoints (anonymous, no key), LLM7.io, AxForge, Cohere (trial key), Cerebras (trial credit), and Ollama and LM Studio locally. Researched in November 2026 from provider docs and two maintained community lists. Each preset says whether it is free, trial, or local, and gives its rules: which models are free (`freeModels`) or paid (`paidModels`), a model-list URL where it is not `/models` (Cohere), known models for providers without a list (`defaultModels`), whether its account id goes in the base URL (Cloudflare), whether a key is optional (LLM7) or absent (OVHcloud), and whether limits apply per model or per account (`limitScope`). Left out: retired, China-only, tiny-allowance, and anonymous-host services.
* **Model sizes.** `modelClass` (core) sorts models into small, medium, and large from the id (parameter count, including mixture-of-experts totals; tier words such as mini, flash-lite, pro, ultra; known large families). `modelScore` ranks within a size.
* **Task levels.** `taskLevel` (core, plain code) gives each run a level:
  * **light:** triage, answer, S-size tasks and their reviews;
  * **standard:** discovery, survey, M-size tasks, reviews and QA of larger tasks, fixes;
  * **heavy:** PRD, design, architecture, plan, scaffold, L-size tasks, integration, repair, the release.

  Each earlier failure of the task raises the level by one (up to two), and customer-facing or regulated stakes keep build work at standard or above.
* **Routing.** `routeModels` orders every working key's usable models for the level: the preferred size first (small for light, medium for standard, large for heavy), best first, at most two per provider and size, then the other sizes as backups, up to twelve. The engine's chain is: a per-role model, then the owner's list for the level (quick for light, main otherwise), then this automatic choice, then the other list. The orchestrator passes the level in `RunSpec` and records it on `run.started`.
* **Per-model limits.** The key pool keeps a cooldown per model as well as per key. A 429 from a per-model provider rests only that model, so the key's other models keep working; per-account providers rest the whole key.
* **No small models for bigger work.** While any medium or large model is available, standard and heavy runs do not fall back to small ones: when the bigger models are at their limits, the run waits for them. In a real project, plan runs that fell back to small and mid-size models looped for over half an hour on the task-file format without producing a plan; waiting costs less than that. With only small models available, they still do everything.
* **Paid models are ruled out, not waited for.** A 402, or a body saying the account needs credits or a balance (OpenRouter's "requires more credits", Z.ai's "Insufficient balance or no resource package", which Z.ai sends as a 429), is a permanent error for that model: it is dropped from the automatic choice and the next model takes over. OpenRouter's daily free cap ("free-models-per-day … Add 10 credits") stays a rate limit. Z.ai's free rule is GLM-4.x Flash only (GLM-5.x Flash and FlashX need a balance), and Settings marks a model in the owner's lists that its provider does not offer for free.
* **Help for models that loop.** `asf plan check` prints the task-file format when a file cannot be read or misses fields, and the engine adds a note to a tool result when the exact same call has failed with the same output three times. Both are plain text in tool output, with no extra model call.

**Consequences.** Routine work runs on small models with large free allowances, and the strongest models are kept for planning, architecture, and hard tasks. A team can add keys from many providers and get their combined allowance. Classification and ranking are heuristics on model ids and list fields: a new model with an unusual name may be classed medium, which is a safe middle. The owner's own lists always come first, and the automatic choice can be turned off. Small models fail more often on complex work; a failed task moves up a level, so a wrong guess costs one run. Provider details (limits, free models, URLs) will keep changing; the presets and their notes are the place to update.
